A credit note reverses some or all of an invoice. It exists because editing an invoice the
customer already has is the wrong fix: their copy and yours stop matching, and if the
invoice has been paid or entered into accounts, the numbers no longer reconcile.

## Raising one

Open the invoice and choose to credit it. You can credit:

- **The whole invoice** — a cancellation.
- **Individual lines** — for example a single item that was returned.
- **An amount** — a goodwill discount after the fact.

The credit note gets its own number and its own document. Both it and the original stay in
the invoice list, which is what an auditor expects to see.

## What it does to the figures

A credit note reduces the amount owing on the invoice it is against. If the invoice was
already paid in full, crediting it leaves a balance in the customer's favour, which you
either refund or set against their next invoice.

The [finance](/using/finance/) figures follow automatically; there is nothing to adjust by
hand.

## When not to use one

If the invoice has not been sent to anyone yet, just edit it. A credit note against an
invoice nobody ever saw is noise in the records for no benefit.
